# Share of people in ICE detention held more than a year

Open since October 9, 2026. From Polar Star Project, a public-interest data page; the figures are counts from public records, never about a person.

## What the records show

- 4,161 people in ICE detention on August 6, 2026 had been held more than a year, and 12,736 more than six months (6.3% and 19.3% of the 65,890 held).
- Long stays often come from cases still before a judge or on appeal, which can take more than a year. ICE's records do not say why any one stay has lasted this long, whether it has been reviewed, or whether the person has a final removal order.
- ICE's own twice-monthly file gives a lower figure, 2,112 held more than a year on June 30, 2026. This count runs from the start of each continuous stay in ICE detention, across transfers; ICE's file does not say how it measures, so the two are not directly comparable.
- For people with a final removal order, the Supreme Court set six months as the presumptively reasonable limit when removal is not reasonably foreseeable (Zadvydas v. Davis, 2001). The records cannot show how many of these stays that applies to; that is the question for the bodies below.
